Chapter 1 — Photosynthesis and the carbon cycle

1.1 Photosynthesis

Green plants use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water. Oxygen is released. Chlorophyll, inside chloroplasts, absorbs the light.

  • Word equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
  • It needs light and chlorophyll.
  • Chlorophyll absorbs mainly red and blue light and reflects green, so leaves look green.
  • Broad, thin leaves catch light and give gases a short path into the cells.
  • The glucose feeds the plant and almost every food chain. The oxygen is used in aerobic respiration.

1.2 More about photosynthesis

The rate is limited by whichever of light, carbon dioxide or temperature is in shortest supply. Glucose may be respired, stored as starch, or built into cellulose, proteins and oils.

Limiting factor

Starch test

  1. Boil the leaf to stop reactions.
  2. Warm it in ethanol to remove chlorophyll. Ethanol is flammable, so a teacher does this step.
  3. Rinse the leaf.
  4. Add iodine. Blue-black means starch is present.

Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll. Nitrate is used to make amino acids and proteins. Stomata are pores that let carbon dioxide in and oxygen and water vapour out.

1.3 The carbon cycle

Carbon atoms are reused. Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide into plants. Feeding moves carbon along food chains. Respiration, decomposition and combustion return carbon dioxide to the air.

Photosynthesis

Removes CO₂ from the air and stores carbon in glucose and other biomass.

Feeding

Animals take in carbon compounds when they eat plants or other animals.

Respiration and decay

Living things and decomposers release CO₂. Decomposers also return minerals to the soil.

Combustion

Burning, including fossil fuels, returns carbon that was stored for a very long time.

A carbon atom does not change into a different element. Over millions of years some remains become fossil fuels or rocks such as limestone.

1.4 Climate change

Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane trap some of the energy Earth radiates. Extra greenhouse gases from human activity trap more heat, so average temperatures and weather patterns shift.

Activities that add greenhouse gases

  • Burning fossil fuels
  • Deforestation
  • Some farming, including methane from livestock and rice paddies

Likely effects

  • Rising sea level as ice melts and seawater expands
  • More extreme weather
  • Habitat loss and pressure on farming

Earth’s climate has changed before. The rapid rise now is linked to carbon that used to be locked in fossil fuels. Trees store carbon while they grow, but they can burn or be cut, so cutting emissions still matters.

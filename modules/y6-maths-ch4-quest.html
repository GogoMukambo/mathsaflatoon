Math Quest: Chapter 4

Decimals

Master ordering, adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing decimals easily.

Why learn this in the real world?

The decimal system is a number system based on 10, which allows us to write all numbers using just ten digits (0-9). Many countries use a decimal system for their currency (e.g., $1 = 100 cents, £1 = 100 pence). Understanding decimals is essential when you travel and exchange money, buy groceries by weight, or compare exact times in competitive sports like the 100m sprint.

1 Ordering Decimals

Comparing Decimals

  • To order decimal numbers, write them from smallest to largest.
  • Different whole-number parts: First compare the whole numbers. (e.g. 14.6 is bigger than 8.9 because 14 > 8).
  • Same whole-number parts: Compare the tenths first, then the hundredths, and so on. (e.g. 2.6 is smaller than 2.82 because 6 tenths is less than 8 tenths).

Adding Trailing Zeros (Ulrika's Method)

  • If numbers have different lengths of decimal places, add zeros at the end to make them the same length. This makes comparing easier.
  • Example: Order 4.23, 4.6, and 4.179. Give them all 3 decimal places: 4.230, 4.600, 4.179. Now compare: 179 < 230 < 600. Ordered: 4.179, 4.23, 4.6.

Symbols & Solved Example

  • < means "is less than".
  • > means "is greater than".
  • ≠ means "is not equal to".

Example: 4.07 < 4.15

Example: 0.183 ≠ 0.18

2 Add & Subtract

Mental Methods

  • Partitioning: Separate into whole numbers and decimals. E.g., 2.3 + 7.8 = (2+7) + (0.3+0.8) = 9 + 1.1 = 10.1.
  • Rounding and Adjusting: If a number is close to a whole, round it, calculate, then adjust. E.g., 6.9 + 12.4. Round 6.9 up to 7. Do 7 + 12.4 = 19.4. Then subtract the extra 0.1 to get 19.3.

Written Methods

  • Always write calculations in columns with the decimal points vertically in line.
  • Fill any empty spaces at the end of a decimal with zeros (e.g. 4.8 becomes 4.80) to help align columns.
  • Add or subtract from right to left, remembering to carry or borrow just like with whole numbers.

Solved Example (Subtracting)

Calculate: 10 - 4.83

  • Mentally: 10 - 4 = 6.
  • 6.0 - 0.8 = 5.2.
  • 5.20 - 0.03 = 5.17.

3 Multiply & Divide

Multiplying & Dividing

  • Multiply: Work out the multiplication without the decimal point. Then count the total decimal places in the question and put that many places in your answer.
  • Divide: Use short division. Keep the decimal point in the answer exactly above the decimal point in the question.

Making Calculations Easier

  • Place Value & Fractions: $0.06 \times 3500$ is the same as $(6 \div 100) \times 3500$, which is $6 \times 35 = 210$.
  • Partitioning: To multiply by 9, multiply by $(10 - 1)$. Example: $8.2 \times 9 = 8.2 \times 10 - 8.2 = 82 - 8.2 = 73.8$.

Solved Examples

Solve mentally: 0.002 × 4

  • Without decimal: 2 × 4 = 8.
  • Three decimal places in question, so three in answer: 0.008.

Divide: 12.56 ÷ 40

  • Divide both by 10 to make it easier: 1.256 ÷ 4.
  • Short division gives 0.314.

Golden Rule

When one integer is positive and the other is negative, the product or quotient is always negative.

Example: -20 ÷ 5 = -4

Lowest Common Multiples (LCM)

The Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of two numbers is the smallest non-zero number that is a multiple of both.

Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12 , 16, 20, 24 ...
Multiples of 6: 6, 12 , 18, 24 , 30...

The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12

Highest Common Factors (HCF)

A factor divides perfectly into another number. The Highest Common Factor (HCF) is the largest common factor between two numbers. It is great for simplifying fractions!

Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9 , 18
Factors of 27: 1, 3, 9 , 27

The HCF of 18 and 27 is 9

Tests for Divisibility

Use these quick rules to check if a large number can be evenly divided!

÷ 2

Last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8.

÷ 3

Sum of all digits is a multiple of 3.

÷ 4

Last two digits are divisible by 4.

÷ 5

Last digit is 0 or 5.

÷ 9

Sum of all digits is a multiple of 9.

÷ 10

Last digit is 0.

Square & Cube Roots

Squares ( x² )

A number multiplied by itself.

  • 2 × 2 = 4
  • 5 × 5 = 25
  • √25 = 5

Cubes ( x³ )

A number multiplied by itself twice.

  • 2 × 2 × 2 = 8
  • 3 × 3 × 3 = 27
  • ∛27 = 3

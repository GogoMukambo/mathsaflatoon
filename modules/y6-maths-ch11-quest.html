1 Functions

A function shows the relationship between two variables. You often use x and y to represent these variables.

Solved Example: Writing a Function

You can exchange 1 US dollar ($1) for 70 Indian rupees (₹70).

  • Let 'x' be US dollars. Let 'y' be Indian rupees.
  • For every 1 unit of x, you get 70 units of y.
  • The relationship is: multiply x by 70 to get y.

Answer: y = 70x

2 Graphs of Functions

You can display values of x and the corresponding values of y in a table, then use these values as coordinates to plot points on a graph.

Solved Example: Plotting y = 0.5x

Show that the point (24, 12) is on the line y = 0.5x, but (15, 30) is not.

  • Test first point (24, 12): When x = 24, y = 0.5 × 24 = 12. Since 12 = 12, this point IS on the line.
  • Test second point (15, 30): When x = 15, y = 0.5 × 15 = 7.5. Since 7.5 ≠ 30, this point is NOT on the line.

3 Lines Parallel to the Axes

A line parallel to the y-axis has the equation x = [number]. A line parallel to the x-axis has the equation y = [number].

Solved Example: Naming lines

A horizontal line passes through -3 on the y-axis. What is its name (equation)?

  • The line is horizontal, so it is parallel to the x-axis.
  • Every point on this line has a y-coordinate of -3.

Answer: y = -3

4 Interpreting Graphs

Graphs show how variables change. A graph sloping upwards has a positive gradient (increase). Sloping downwards is a negative gradient (decrease). Horizontal is zero gradient.

Solved Example: Rate of Change

A straight-line graph shows Cost ($) vs Time (min). At 4 minutes, the cost is $1.60. What is the rate per minute?

  • The graph is a straight line starting at 0, meaning the rate is constant.
  • Divide total cost by total time: $1.60 ÷ 4 minutes.

Answer: $0.40 per minute

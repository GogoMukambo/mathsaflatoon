Math Quest: Chapter 8

Shapes & Symmetry

Master the properties of 2D & 3D shapes, circles, symmetry, and congruence.

Why learn this in the real world?

Symmetry has been an important part of the design of everyday objects throughout history, seen in architecture like the Alhambra Palace in Spain. Understanding the properties of 2D and 3D shapes helps architects, engineers, and designers visualize and construct buildings, packaging, and art with perfect precision and structural integrity.

Chapter Modules:

  • Identifying lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry in 2D shapes.
  • Understanding circles (radius, diameter, chord, tangent).
  • Describing properties of regular and irregular polygons.
  • Recognizing congruent shapes (identical in shape and size).
  • Visualizing 3D shapes (faces, vertices, edges, and front/top/side views).

1 Symmetry & Circles

8.1 2D Symmetry

  • Line Symmetry: A line where you can fold a shape and both halves match exactly (also called a mirror line). A rectangle has 2.
  • Rotational Symmetry: The number of times a shape looks exactly the same during one full 360° turn. A shape that only fits on itself once has order 1. A rectangle has order 2.

8.2 Circles & Polygons

  • Chord: A straight line starting and finishing on the circumference. (A diameter is a special chord that passes through the centre).
  • Tangent: A straight line that touches the outside of the circle at exactly one point. The angle between a tangent and a radius is always 90°.
  • Regular Polygons: All sides and angles are equal. E.g., a regular octagon has 8 equal sides, 8 equal angles, 8 lines of symmetry, and rotational order 8.

2 Congruent Shapes

8.3 Recognising Congruent Shapes

  • Shapes that are identical in shape and size are called congruent.
  • They might be in a different orientation (rotated or flipped), but if you trace one, it will fit exactly over the other.
  • In congruent shapes, corresponding sides are equal in length, and corresponding angles are equal in size.
  • Congruent shapes will always have the exact same perimeter and the exact same area.

Solved Example

Triangle ABC is congruent to triangle DEF. AC = 13cm, angle BAC = 35°. Find DF and angle EDF.

  • AC and DF are corresponding sides. So DF = 13cm.
  • Angle BAC and angle EDF are corresponding angles. So angle EDF = 35°.

3 3D Shapes & Views

8.4 3D Shapes Properties

  • Faces: The flat surfaces of a solid shape.
  • Edges: The straight lines where two faces meet.
  • Vertices: The corners where edges meet.
  • E.g., A Cuboid has 6 rectangular faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices.

Visualizing Views (Elevations)

You must be able to draw what a 3D shape looks like from different 2D perspectives:

  • Top view (Plan): Looking straight down from above.
  • Front view: Looking directly at the front face.
  • Side view: Looking directly from the side.
  • E.g., A cylinder looks like a circle from the top, but a rectangle from the front or side.

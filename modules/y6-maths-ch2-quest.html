1 Expressions & Formulae

2.1 Constructing Expressions

  • In algebra you can use a letter to represent an unknown number.
  • An expression contains numbers and letters, but not an equals sign.
  • In the expression 5n + 4, there are two terms. The letter n is the variable. The coefficient of n is 5 because it is the number that multiplies the variable.

2.2 Using Formulae

A formula is a mathematical rule that shows the relationship between two quantities (variables) and has an equals sign.

  • For example, Area of a rectangle: A = l × w
  • You can substitute numbers into an expression or formula to work out the value.

Solved Examples

Work out the value of: 2w - 3v when w = 12 and v = 5

  • Substitute 12 for w and 5 for v in the expression.
  • Use the correct order of operations: 2 × 12 - 3 × 5.
  • 24 - 15 = 9.

Write a formula for the number of days in any number of weeks.

  • There are seven days in a week, so multiply the number of weeks by 7.
  • Choose d for days and w for weeks. Answer: d = 7w.

2 Terms & Brackets

2.3 Collecting Like Terms

  • Like terms are terms that contain the same letter.
  • You can add, subtract or combine like terms (e.g. 2x + 3x = 5x).
  • You cannot combine terms that contain different letters. This means you rewrite the expression in as short a way as possible.

2.4 Expanding Brackets

  • To expand a term with brackets, you multiply each term inside the brackets by the term outside the brackets.
  • Expanding a term with brackets is sometimes called 'multiplying out the brackets'.

Solved Examples

Simplify: 4p + 3q + 2p - q

  • 4p and 2p are like terms, so add them to get 6p.
  • 3q and -q are like terms, so subtract to get 2q.
  • 6p and 2q are not like terms so you cannot simplify any further: 6p + 2q.

Expand the brackets: 3(2g + h - 7)

  • Multiply the 3 by the 2g, then multiply the 3 by the h, then the 3 by the -7.
  • Answer: 6g + 3h - 21.

3 Equations & Inequalities

2.5 Solving Equations

  • When you solve an equation, you find the value of the unknown letter.
  • You can use inverse operations. For example, if you have x + 5 = 12, subtract 5 from both sides of the equation.
  • Work out the value of x, then substitute this value back into the equation to check that the answer is correct.

2.6 Understanding Inequalities

You can use a letter and an inequality to represent an open interval.

  • < means 'is less than' and > means 'is greater than'.
  • The inequality x > 4 means that x can be any number greater than 4.
  • You use an open circle for < and > inequalities on a number line.

Solved Examples

Solve the equation: 2y + 4 = 16

  • Subtract 4 from both sides: 2y = 12.
  • Divide both sides by 2: y = 6.
  • Check: 2 × 6 + 4 = 12 + 4 = 16.

List the integer values that x could be for x > 5

  • x is greater than 5, so 6 is the smallest number.
  • The list is 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, ...

Probability

Master probability scales, outcomes, and experimental probability.

1 The Probability Scale

Probability is a number between 0 and 1 representing the likelihood of an event. 0 is impossible, 0.5 is an even chance, and 1 is certain.

Solved Example: Comparing Probabilities

The probability Tigers win is 45%. The probability Lions win is 4/5. Which is more likely to win?

  • Convert Lions probability to a percentage: 4/5 = 0.8 = 80%.
  • Compare the percentages: 80% is greater than 45%.

Answer: Lions are more likely to win.

2 Mutually Exclusive Outcomes

When two outcomes cannot happen at the same time they are mutually exclusive. If outcomes are equally likely (like rolling a fair dice), you can calculate theoretical probabilities.

Solved Example: Theoretical Probability

There are 10 cards numbered 2, 5, 8, 5, 9, 8, 5, 3, 8, 5. What is the probability of choosing a 5?

  • There are 10 cards in total, so each card has a 1/10 chance of being chosen.
  • Four cards have the number 5.
  • The probability is 4/10.
  • Simplified, this is 2/5 or 40%.

Answer: 40%

3 Experimental Probabilities

When outcomes are not equally likely, you can use an experiment to estimate probabilities by calculating the relative frequency: (Frequency of outcome / Total number of trials).

Solved Example: Relative Frequency

124 drawing pins are dropped. 48 land point up. Find the experimental probability of a pin landing point up.

  • The frequency of point up is 48.
  • The total number of trials is 124.
  • The relative frequency is 48 / 124 = 0.39 (to 2 decimal places).

Answer: 39%

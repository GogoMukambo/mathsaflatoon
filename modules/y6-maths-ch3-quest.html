Math Quest: Chapter 3

Place Value & Rounding

Master powers of 10, multiplying, dividing, and rounding to decimal places.

Why learn this?

Numbers are often rounded in everyday situations because they are easier to work with and compare. For example, market researchers use exact numbers in calculations but round their final answers to more easily compare large-scale data, like national populations or phone usage.

What you will learn:

  • Multiply and divide whole numbers by powers of 10.
  • Multiply and divide decimals by powers of 10.
  • Round numbers to a given number of decimal places.

1 Powers of 10

Understanding Indices

  • You can write numbers like 10, 100, 1000, 10000 as powers of 10.
  • The power of 10 is written as an index. This is the number of 10s you multiply together to get the final number.
  • It is also the same as the number of zeros that follow the digit 1. For example, 100,000 has 5 zeros, so it is 105.

Multiplying & Dividing Rules

  • When you multiply a number by a power of 10, you move the digits to the left in the place value table.
  • When you divide a number by a power of 10, you move the digits to the right in the place value table.

Solved Examples

Work out: 23 × 104

  • 104 is the same as 10,000.
  • Move digits four places left. The empty spaces are filled with zeros.
  • Answer: 230,000.

Work out: 45,000 ÷ 105

  • 105 is the same as 100,000.
  • Move digits five places to the right.
  • Answer: 0.45.

2 Decimals & Powers

Classroom Theory

  • The rule of moving digits in the place value table applies to decimal numbers just as it does for whole numbers.
  • When multiplying a decimal by a power of 10, the decimal point appears to move to the right.
  • When dividing a decimal by a power of 10, the decimal point appears to move to the left.

Converting Metric Units

Powers of 10 are very useful for metric conversions.

  • Number of mm = number of cm × 10
  • Number of mm = number of m × 103
  • Number of mm = number of km × 106

Solved Example

Convert 8,000,000 mm to km.

  • Start by converting mm to m: 8,000,000 ÷ 103 = 8,000 m.
  • Then convert m to km: 8,000 ÷ 103 = 8 km.
  • Or apply the reverse formula directly: km = mm ÷ 106.

Answer: 8 km.

3 Rounding

Classroom Theory

  • When you are asked to round a number, you will be told how accurate your answer should be. This is the degree of accuracy.
  • Look at the digit in the position of the required degree of accuracy. What you do depends on the value of the digit to its right.

The Rounding Rule

  • If the value of the digit to its right is 5 or more, increase the original digit by 1.
  • If the value of the digit to its right is less than 5, leave the original digit as it is.

Solved Examples

Round the number 5.376398 to one decimal place (1 d.p.)

  • The digit in the first decimal place is 3.
  • The digit to the right of the 3 is 7.
  • 7 is more than 5, so round the 3 up to 4. Answer: 5.4.

Round the number 5.376398 to three decimal places (3 d.p.)

  • The digit in the third decimal place is 6. The digit to its right is 3.
  • 3 is less than 5, so the 6 stays the same. Answer: 5.376.

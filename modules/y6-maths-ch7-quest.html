7 Fractions

Master ordering, adding mixed numbers, and multiplying/dividing fractions.

Why learn this in the real world?

The word ‘fraction’ comes from the Latin fractio — breaking into pieces. You use fractions for shop discounts, recipes, and distance signs every day.

Chapter modules

  • Compare and order fractions (common denominators or decimals).
  • Add mixed numbers efficiently.
  • Multiply and divide proper fractions.
  • Simplify calculations using factors and equivalent fractions.

7 Fractions › 7.1 Ordering fractions

1 7.1 Ordering fractions

  • Compare fractions by giving them a common denominator.
  • Or write them as decimals (divide numerator by denominator).
  • Recurring decimals repeat forever (e.g. 0.6̇).
  • Change improper fractions to mixed numbers before comparing.

Solved example

Order from smallest: 2.6, 2.816, 2.82

  • All have whole part 2 — compare tenths: 2.6 has 6 tenths (smallest so far).
  • 2.816 has 1 hundredth; 2.82 has 2 hundredths.
  • Order: 2.6, 2.816, 2.82.

Compare: ⅔ and ¾ using twelfths → 8/12 < 9/12.

2 7.2 Adding mixed numbers

  • Step 1: Add whole-number parts for an estimate.
  • Step 2: Add fractional parts with a common denominator; simplify.
  • Step 3: Combine whole numbers and fractions for the final answer.

Solved example

Work out: 2¼ + 3⅖

  • Add whole numbers: 2 + 3 = 5.
  • Add fractions: ¼ + ⅖ = 3/12 + 10/12 = 13/12 = 1&frac112;.
  • Combine: 5 + 1&frac112; = 6&frac112;.

2 Multiply & Divide

7.3 Multiplying Fractions

  • To find a fraction of a fraction, multiply them together.
  • Multiply the numerators together, and multiply the denominators together.
  • Always cancel your final answer to its simplest form.

7.4 Dividing Fractions

  • Step 1: Turn the second fraction upside down (this gives you the reciprocal).
  • Step 2: Multiply the fractions together, as usual.
  • Step 3: Write the answer in its simplest form, and as a mixed number when possible.

Solved Examples

Multiply: ⅖ × ⅞

  • Multiply top and bottom: (2×7) / (5×8) = 14 / 40.
  • Simplify (divide by 2): 7 / 20.

Divide: ⅖ ÷ 3/10

  • Turn second upside down and multiply: ⅖ × 10/3.
  • (2×10) / (5×3) = 20 / 15.
  • Simplify and convert: 4 / 3 = 1⅓.

3 Making Calculations Easier

7.5 Using Factors & Equivalence

  • Break a fraction into parts using factors. E.g., ¼ is ½ × ½. So ¼ of 600 means halving 600, then halving it again.
  • Use equivalent fractions. E.g., ⅗ × 120 is easier as 6/10 × 120. (120 ÷ 10 = 12, then 12 × 6 = 72).

Cancelling Same Factors

  • Find factors that are the same in the numerator and denominator to cancel them out before multiplying.

Solved Examples

Solve: ⅞ × ⅘

  • Replace 8 with 2 × 4: (7 × 4) / (2 × 4 × 5).
  • Rearrange to cancel the 4s: (7 × 4) / (10 × 4).
  • Cancel 4/4 to leave 7/10.

Solve: 1/14 × 700

  • Split 1/14 into 1/7 × ½.
  • 700 ÷ 7 = 100. Then 100 ÷ 2 = 50.

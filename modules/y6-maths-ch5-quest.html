Angles & Constructions

Master the properties of angles, intersecting lines, and accurate shape construction.

Why learn this in the real world?

Angles and geometric constructions are the foundation of navigation, architecture, and design. Whether an engineer is designing a safe bridge, a pilot is calculating flight paths, or a carpenter is building furniture, they rely on exact angle sums (like 180° and 360°) and the rules of parallel and intersecting lines to ensure structures fit together perfectly and safely.

1 A sum of 360°

Line = 180°
Point = 360°

Core Angle Rules

  • The sum of the angles on a straight line is 180°.
  • A whole turn is 360°. The sum of the angles around a point is 360°.
  • The sum of the angles of a triangle is 180°.
  • The sum of the angles of a quadrilateral (a shape with 4 straight sides) is 360°.

Using Algebra

You can apply algebra skills to find unknown angles represented by letters. Form an equation based on the geometric rule.

  • Example: If angles around a point are 142°, 77°, and a, then 142 + 77 + a = 360.

2 Intersecting Lines

Opposite Angles
Parallel Lines

Opposite & Perpendicular

  • When two straight lines intersect, the vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Adjacent angles on the intersecting lines form a straight line, meaning they add up to 180°.
  • If lines intersect and the angle between them is exactly 90°, they are perpendicular lines.

Parallel Lines & Transversals

  • Lines that never intersect are parallel. Arrows on lines show they are parallel.
  • A line crossing parallel lines is called a transversal. It creates matching sets of equal angles (corresponding, alternate).

3 Drawing & Constructions

Perpendicular (90°)
Protractor Tool

Using Geometry Tools

  • You can use a ruler, a protractor, or a set square to make accurate drawings.
  • To draw a perpendicular line, put one edge of a set square on your original line, and draw along the other edge. Or, use a protractor to mark exactly 90°.
  • To draw a parallel line at a specific distance (e.g., 5cm), measure 5cm up a perpendicular line and draw across.

Drawing Quadrilaterals

You don't need to know all sides and angles to draw a quadrilateral, just a specific set of measurements.

  • Step 1: Draw a base line of a known length.
  • Step 2: Place a protractor at the vertex and mark the known angle. Draw the side to its exact length.
  • Step 3: Repeat for other known angles/sides, and connect the final vertices.
